# Frontier Direction Discovery

Choose a field worth sustained work and a credible way to encounter it. Do not name the
final paper before empirical contact.

## Select For Consequence And Contact

Prefer programs with:

- clear scientific or practical value and an active top-venue community;
- accepted benchmarks or real workflows with inspectable residual behavior;
- strong published methods and reproducible implementations;
- feasible access to models, data, evaluation, and enough compute for meaningful
  contact;
- a consequential shared tension, tradeoff, or assumption not exhausted by headline
  metrics;
- room for a simple reusable principle that could alter downstream design and survive
  model-generation change.

Crowdedness is evidence of importance and evaluation maturity only when activity serves
a real value. It raises the baseline and differentiation burden; it does not close the
field. Conversely, an untouched topic is not valuable merely because it is novel.

Search recent accepted papers, proceedings, official repositories, benchmark documents,
leaderboards, appendices, and implementation issues to learn what outcomes the community
trusts, which methods recur, what tradeoffs they make, and what users still cannot rely
on. A limitation paragraph is a lead to test, not an established project premise.

## Choose The Right Research Scale

Select a mesoscopic arena recognizable to an active community: narrower than an entire
field but broader than one model, metric, layer, dataset cell, or proposed module. The
arena should have one canonical input-to-output or training object and one primary value
surface. Its best-case result should change a community decision rather than merely
improve a private slice.

Check feasibility without shrinking ambition. Distinguish a phenomenon visible at the
available scale, a frontier-only question needing different resources, an inaccessible
implementation, and a feasible program whose first experiment is poor. Do not replace a
difficult method question with a benchmark-only, prompt-only, or analysis-only project
for convenience.

## Record A Program, Not A Predetermined Method

Record the selected program in the project's research-program authority:

- the field and why it matters;
- the accepted empirical surface and primary outcome;
- strong recent published incumbents and implementation anchors;
- a non-binding lens for what behavior to inspect;
- resource, data, contribution, and user-owned boundaries;
- the first empirical contact.

The lens may guide saved traces or comparisons. It cannot dictate the natural failure,
mechanism, final method, stopping rule, or paper identity.

When generating several folders from a sparse portfolio brief, do not duplicate this
selection logic in an ad hoc prompt. Read
[project-generation.md](project-generation.md), research the current venue and frontier,
produce the validated manifest, and let the deterministic renderer create accepted
projects. The requested project count is a ceiling, not a quota.

## Return A Decision

When the user is choosing among fields, return a small ranked set with value, accepted
surface, representative methods, timeliness, non-binding lens, method-level opportunity,
resource reality, main risk, and first contact. Recommend one bet and explain why.

Once selected, update the authoritative project state with primary-source evidence and
begin empirical contact. Preserve the user's seed verbatim and label the selection
rationale as interpretation. Direction discovery does not grant a paper identity or
terminate a live empirical program from literature alone.

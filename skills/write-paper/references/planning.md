# Paper planning checklist

## Contribution identity

Write one line for each:

- problem;
- positive object;
- contribution type: method, analysis, benchmark, system, theory, or mixed;
- load-bearing primitive or thesis;
- claimed population or standard task;
- primary metric;
- decisive baseline;
- paper-to-program bridge.

## Section contract

For every section, record:

- question the section answers;
- claims introduced;
- evidence or citation required;
- figure/table dependency;
- strongest likely reviewer objection;
- maximum reasonable length.

## Figure-first plan

Each planned figure should have:

- one scientific question;
- one intended takeaway;
- source data path;
- comparison set;
- uncertainty or sample definition;
- the claim IDs it supports.

## Introduction test

The introduction should make clear, in order:

1. what consequential capability or understanding is missing;
2. why existing approaches do not resolve it;
3. the key empirical or conceptual insight;
4. the proposed primitive or thesis;
5. the strongest evidence;
6. the precise contributions and scope.

Avoid opening with generic claims about rapid progress or broad importance unless
they directly set up the paper's concrete problem.
